## 1. Feasibility verdict — which tenders can WE actually fulfill?

I read the actual solicitation documents (not just the notices). Most federal tenders contain
**mandatory corporate-experience criteria** that screen out new vendors regardless of capability.
Here is the honest breakdown:

### ✅ Winnable NOW (no corporate track record required)

| Tender | Why it's winnable | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **IRB Designated Representative Services** (sol. 20252716-IRB) — up to **100 contracts** | Application is a personal questionnaire: 6 mandatory behavioural questions (max 350 words each) + resume + proof of age (18+) and Canadian citizenship / legal work status. **No company history required.** Point-rated bonus for experience with minors, mental-health populations, addictions. Oral interview follows. | **June 30, 2026, 5 PM** |

Work: representing vulnerable individuals (minors, persons unable to appreciate proceedings) in
IRB immigration/refugee hearings, in Montreal/Ottawa/Atlantic, Toronto/GTA, or Vancouver/Calgary/
Edmonton/Winnipeg regions. Any team member with client-facing professional experience
(social work, education, healthcare, HR, community services) can apply individually.

**Important — individual contracts only.** The NPP and application form make this a
person-by-person procurement: the applicant's own resume, proof of age, proof of
citizenship/work status, first-person behavioural answers, and a personal oral interview.
A company cannot bid to supply a team; assignments are distributed among qualified DRs
individually. The "team play" is parallel applications by each qualified person — contracts
and revenue belong to those individuals, not the firm. (Open question to ask
dr.rd@irb-cisr.gc.ca before bidding: whether an individual may contract through their
personal corporation.) For a true vendor/prime model where the company holds the contract,
target instead: StatCan ESL training, AVAYA training, Translation RFSA, Parks Canada
Exhibits RFSA.

*build references commercially, then qualify for the evergreen RFSAs* (Media Monitoring is open

## 2. Execution plan

### Phase 0 — Registrations (this week, June 10–13)

1. Register the business in **CanadaBuys / SAP Ariba** and obtain a **Procurement Business Number (PBN)** (free; needed for any federal bid).
2. Confirm CRA Business Number, liability insurance status, and bilingual capacity inventory of the team.
3. Sign up for CanadaBuys **email alerts** on the categories below (translation, e-learning, media analysis, training services).

*AI support:* registration walkthrough, GSIN/UNSPSC category selection, alert keyword design.

### Phase 1 — Win IRB Designated Representative contracts (June 10–30)

Per applicant (you + each suitable employee — every qualified person is a separate contract chance):

| Step | When | AI role |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory real client-facing experience (jobs, volunteering, advocacy) | Jun 10–12 | Structured interview to surface usable, *truthful* examples |
| Draft 6 mandatory answers (1A trust/communication, 1B empathy/advocacy, 1C mental-health awareness, 2A client needs, 3A planning/deadlines) in STAR format, ≤350 words, naming employer/role/responsibilities as required | Jun 12–18 | Draft, tighten to word limit, check each answer hits every sub-element of the criterion |
| Draft point-rated answers (4A tribunal-setting representation; 4B addictions / mental-health / minors experience) — only where genuinely applicable | Jun 12–18 | Same |
| Polish resume to mirror the criteria vocabulary | Jun 18 | Rewrite/format |
| Assemble 4 attachments (questionnaire, resume, proof of age, proof of status) and email to dr.rd@irb-cisr.gc.ca | **by Jun 25** (buffer before Jun 30 17:00) | Compliance checklist |
| Oral interview prep (questions are drawn from the written questionnaire) | On invite | Mock-interview Q&A drills |

**Integrity rule:** every example must be real and reference-checkable — the IRB reserves the right
to call references. AI drafts wording; it never invents experience.

### Phase 2 — Build qualifying references (July–December 2026)

Pick **one** service line and get 2 paying reference projects (private sector, nonprofits, school
boards, municipalities — MASH-sector and commercial work counts as "projects" for most federal
mandatory criteria):

- **Option A – AI-assisted media monitoring & analysis:** stand up a monitoring stack (commercial
  API + AI analysis layer producing daily briefs with tone/source/region coverage). Sell to 2
  municipal/association clients. After ~2 years this satisfies Media Monitoring RFSA Stream 1/2
  M-criteria; meanwhile, pursue **subcontracting to current SA holders** to learn the delivery format.
- **Option B – Bilingual e-learning/quiz content:** build a WCAG 2.0 AA, EN/FR quiz-and-course
  platform and deliver 2 education-sector projects. This directly manufactures eligibility for the
  IRCC Secondary School Engagement type of RFP (it recurs) and the COTS E-Learning RFSO
  (open to Sep 2027).
- **Option C – Translation services management:** contract 2–3 CTTIC-certified translators
  (EN↔FR), implement an AI-assisted translation + certified-human-revision pipeline, and submit a
  Translation RFSA tier qualification (quarterly evaluation windows; verify the SA's machine-
  translation and confidentiality clauses before relying on AI in delivery).

*AI role in delivery:* content drafting, quiz/course generation, summarization and tone analysis,
EN↔FR draft translation for human revision, report automation, WCAG-compliance checking.

### Phase 3 — Qualify for evergreen vehicles (Q4 2026 – 2027)

1. Submit **Translation RFSA** qualification (once Option C resources signed).
2. Submit **Parks Canada Exhibits RFSA** for the writing/content-development à-la-carte streams.
3. Submit **COTS E-Learning RFSO** entry (if Option B catalogue exists).
4. Re-bid recurring RFPs (media monitoring task contracts, training services) as references mature.
5. Monitor TBIPS/TSPS supply-arrangement **refresh windows** for IT/professional staffing entry.

### Ongoing — Pipeline discipline

- Weekly: re-run the CanadaBuys open-data scan (the analysis pipeline used for this assessment is
  repeatable) and triage new notices against our capability matrix.
- Maintain a **bid library** (certifications, project descriptions, resumes, past answers) so each
  new bid is an assembly job, not a writing job.
- Track win/loss and request debriefs from contracting authorities after every loss (a supplier
  right — debriefs reveal exactly what the winning technical scores looked like).

## 3. Risks & guardrails

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Mandatory criteria misread → non-responsive bid | Build a compliance matrix per bid; every "must" gets a cross-referenced page number |
| AI-generated claims drifting from reality | Human owner signs off every factual statement; AI is drafter/checker only |
| Security requirements (Reliability status) | Triggered post-award via the Contract Security Program; sponsorship usually comes with the contract — flag any bid requiring pre-existing clearance |
| Confidential/protected data in AI tools | Never put IRB case data or Protected-B material into uncontrolled AI tools; delivery workflows must respect the contract's confidentiality clauses |
| Indigenous set-aside (PSIB) conditions | Check each notice; conditionally set-aside tenders (e.g., RCMP court reporting) may never open to general bidders |
| Pricing risk on Highest-Combined-Rating (70/30, 60/40) evaluations | Price to win the technical score first; the prorated price formula punishes only large gaps |
